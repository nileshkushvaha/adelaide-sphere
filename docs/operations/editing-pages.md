# Editing pages

A guide for the people who write and publish pages in the admin. No technical
knowledge is needed. It covers Website → Pages (change log 1.17).

## What a page is made of

A page is a stack of **sections**, top to bottom. Each section is a ready-made
block that always looks right on computers and phones:

| Section | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Page header | The big title at the top, a short introduction, an optional picture and a button. Only one, always first. |
| Text | Paragraphs, headings, lists, links and tables, written like a document. |
| Image and text | A picture beside a heading and a few paragraphs. |
| Call to action | A highlighted band asking visitors to do one thing, with one or two buttons. |
| Feature cards | Two to six short cards with an icon, a title and a sentence. |
| Questions and answers | Common questions that open to show their answers. |
| Businesses | Up to six published businesses from the directory. |
| Contact | The site's contact details, with the enquiry form if you want it. |

The Privacy Policy, Terms of Use and Review Guidelines are kept plain: they can
use only the page header, text, questions and contact sections.

## Creating a page

1. Go to **Website → Pages** and choose **New page**.
2. Pick a starting point. *Information page* suits most pages; *Service or
   landing page* gives you a header, cards, image and text, questions and a
   call to action. You can add or remove sections afterwards.
3. Type the **title**. The web address is suggested from it; choose **Edit**
   under the title to write your own.
4. Fill in the sections. Grey text in an empty box is an example, not content —
   it is never published.
5. Choose **Create page**. The page is saved as a draft; nobody outside the
   admin can see it yet.

## Working with sections

- **Open or close** a section by clicking its name.
- **Reorder** by dragging the handle on the left, or use the up and down arrows.
- **Hide** a section to keep it without showing it to visitors.
- **Duplicate** copies a section just below it.
- **Remove** deletes it; choose **Undo** in the message that appears if that was
  a mistake.
- **Add section** at the bottom shows every section you can use, with a
  sentence about each.

Pictures must come from the Media library and have a description (alt text).
Buttons can go to a page on this site (for example `/contact`), a full web
address, an email (`mailto:`), a phone number (`tel:`), or a PDF from the Media
library.

## The Publish box

The box on the right says where the page stands and what is left to do.

- **Not ready to publish** lists what to fix — for example "add some text" or
  "choose a picture". It updates as you type and is checked again in full when
  you save.
- **Save draft** / **Update page** saves your changes. On a published page,
  saving updates the live page within a minute.
- **Publish** makes the page live. It waits until your changes are saved and the
  checklist is clear.
- **Schedule** publishes at a date and time you choose, in Melbourne time, even
  if nobody is signed in. If the page no longer passes the checklist at that
  time, it goes back to draft and says why.
- **Unpublish** takes the page down. You are asked why; the reason is kept in
  the activity log. Nothing on the page is lost.

## Preview

**Preview** opens the page in the real site design in a new tab, including
changes you have not saved yet. The preview link is private, works for ten
minutes, and stops working if you sign out. Search engines never see it.

A page must be saved once before it can be previewed.

## Changes are kept safe

- **Unsaved changes** are copied privately every few seconds while you work. If
  your browser closes or you lose connection, open the page again and choose
  **Restore them**.
- **History** lists every saved version with who saved it and when. Choose one
  to see what changed, and **Restore this version** to bring it back. The page
  as it was before is kept as a version too, so a restore can be undone.
- If someone else saved the page while you were editing, you are told before
  anything is overwritten. Copy what you need, then reload.

## Search and sharing

The **Search & sharing** tab controls how the page appears in Google and when
shared on social media: the title and description shown in results, keywords,
and a share image. **Search advice** suggests improvements; it never stops you
publishing.

**Hide from search engines** keeps the page working for anyone with the link
but asks search engines not to list it, and leaves it out of the sitemap. Use it
for pages such as a thank-you page.

## Changing a page's address

Under the address, choose **Change address** (you need permission to publish).

- If the page is **published**, anyone using the old address — a bookmark, a
  shared link or a search result — is sent to the new one automatically.
- Menus that link the page follow it on their own.
- The Privacy Policy, Terms of Use and Review Guidelines cannot move, because
  the site links to them by address.

Save your changes before changing the address.

## Duplicating a page

In the pages list, choose **Duplicate** on a page to copy it into a new draft
with its own title and address. Useful when a new service page should look like
an existing one.

## Linking a page from the header or footer

Pages are not added to the header or footer automatically. Once a page is
published, add it in **Website → Menus**.

## Permissions

What you can do depends on the permissions your administrator gave you:

| Permission | Allows |
| --- | --- |
| Pages · View | See pages, preview them and read their history |
| Pages · Create | Create and duplicate pages |
| Pages · Update | Edit, save, restore versions and keep unsaved changes |
| Pages · Publish | Publish, schedule, unpublish and change addresses |
| Pages · Delete | Delete pages you created (unpublish them first) |
